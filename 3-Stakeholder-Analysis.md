# Stakeholder Analysis
## Inventory Rebalancing Strategy - Meridian Home Goods

---

## 1. Stakeholder Map

| Stakeholder | Role | Power | Interest | Engagement Approach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VP Merchandising (Executive Sponsor) | Approves budget, owns category performance | High | High | Monthly steering update framed in ARR/margin terms |
| Head of Supply Chain & Planning | Owns reorder-point policy and replenishment cadence | High | High | Weekly working sessions during design and pilot |
| Category Managers (5, one per category) | Own product mix and supplier relationships per category | Medium | High | Individually briefed before pilot; direct input on which SKUs to rebalance first |
| Store Operations Managers (4 stores) | Execute shelf replenishment and report local stock issues | Medium | Medium | Informed at rollout; feedback loop during pilot |
| Finance | Tracks inventory investment and working-capital metrics | Medium | Medium | Informed via business case sign-off and quarterly reviews |
| IT / Data Team | Maintains POS and inventory data feeds the model depends on | Medium | Low | Involved from Planning phase to confirm data availability |

## 2. RACI Matrix

| Activity | VP Merch | Supply Chain Head | Category Mgrs | Store Ops | Finance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Business case approval | A | C | I | I | C |
| Strategy sign-off | A | R | C | I | I |
| Requirements (BRD) sign-off | I | A | C | I | I |
| Reorder-point model build | I | A | C | I | I |
| Pilot SKU/category selection | C | A | R | I | I |
| Store rollout & training | I | C | I | A | I |
| Pilot go/no-go decision | A | R | C | C | I |
| Full rollout | I | R | R | R | I |

*R = Responsible, A = Accountable, C = Consulted, I = Informed*

## 3. Power/Interest Grid Summary

- **Manage Closely (high power, high interest):** VP Merchandising, Head of Supply Chain & Planning — the primary decision-makers whose sign-off and teams drive execution.
- **Keep Satisfied (high power, lower interest):** Finance, IT/Data — need visibility on capital impact and data dependencies but aren't running day-to-day execution.
- **Keep Informed (lower power, high interest):** Category Managers — directly affected by allocation changes to "their" SKUs, a key source of ground-truth pushback and validation.
- **Monitor (lower power, lower interest):** Store Operations Managers beyond rollout — critical during training and go-live, less involved once the model is running.

## 4. Engagement Cadence

- **Steering (VP Merch, Supply Chain Head):** Monthly, aligned to phase gates in the [Project Lifecycle](./4-Project-Lifecycle.md)
- **Working team (Supply Chain, Category Managers, IT/Data):** Weekly during Design and Build
- **Store Operations Managers:** One training session before pilot start, then a standing feedback channel during the pilot window
